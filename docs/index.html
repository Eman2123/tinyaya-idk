Teaching TinyAya to say "I don't know"

Results from 61 runs of CohereLabs/tiny-aya-global: the base model plus five fine-tuning recipes in English, Spanish, Arabic and Malay. Test split, 252 MKQA questions per language. Recipes use 3 seeds; the base model is one deterministic run.

Code and write-up on GitHub Adapters on Hugging Face Data and outputs on Hugging Face

Each bar is all test questions, split three ways. A good abstaining model turns red (wrong answers) into grey (abstained) without losing much green (correct).

Over-refusal is the share of questions the base model answered correctly that the tuned model refused. Abstain recall is the share of questions the base model got wrong that the tuned model refused. Values are means over 3 seeds, with standard deviation under hallucination.

Is a probe as good as gold labels?

Paired bootstrap on the test questions, outcomes averaged over seeds. A filled dot means the 95% interval excludes zero. A hollow dot means we cannot tell the two recipes apart.

R4 (probe labels, no gold labels in the target language) matches R2 (gold labels) in English, Spanish and Malay. In Arabic R4 is more conservative: 0.040 less hallucination, but also 0.021 fewer correct answers. Against R1 (teacher traps) R4 cuts hallucination by 0.32 to 0.52 in every language.

Does abstention carry over to another language?

Rows are the language an adapter was trained on, columns the language it is tested on. The outlined diagonal is same-language. Seed 1 only.

Transfer is partial. English-trained adapters help most on Spanish and Malay (about 0.35 less hallucination than the base model); Arabic and the non-English pairs move much less. Base-model hallucination is 0.80 to 0.85 everywhere, so anything near that is no transfer at all.

How good are the labels that train R3 and R4?

AUROC for telling known from unknown questions, with 95% intervals. The probe is trained on English gold labels and transferred; consistency needs at least 4 of 5 sampled answers to agree.

Probe (R4)Consistency (R3)

The intervals overlap in every language, so the fair reading is "comparable or better", not "clearly better". Precision of the known class is only 0.40 to 0.48 for both sources.

What these numbers do not show

  • One QA source (MKQA, translated questions) and small training sets, 238 to 382 examples.
  • The probe trains on English gold labels, so the method is gold-free only in the target languages.
  • Over-refusal is high: R4 refuses 19 to 38 percent of questions the base model answered correctly (19 to 49 percent across R2 to R5).
  • Refusal templates and few-shot prompts in Spanish, Arabic and Malay were not checked by native speakers.
  • The transfer matrix is one seed, and the published adapters are seed 1 only.
  • The safety check uses 8 English prompts and found no actionable harmful answers. It is not a safety evaluation.
